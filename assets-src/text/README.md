## Edits from the 1902 transcription to the adapted Spanish

The owner's rule (ADR 0002): nothing explicit or racist, in either language; the Spanish follows Lang's 28 paragraphs so a language switch keeps the reader's place (contract K02). Every change is listed so it can be reviewed against the scan.

**Spelling.** Accents brought to current rules, words untouched: *á, é, ó* as single-letter words → *a, e, o*; *fué, vió* → *fue, vio*; *heróica* → *heroica*; *oir* → *oír*; *sólo* (adverb) → *solo*; *aquél, éste, ésta* (pronouns) → *aquel, este, esta*. Unbalanced « » around dialogue replaced by the dash (raya) alone. Printing errors fixed: *le* Gran Tartaria → *la*; *quc* → *que*; *juzgareis* → *juzgaréis*; the heroine's name normalized to *Gerenarda* (also printed *Generarda*), and *Chazenan* → *Chasenan*.

**Cut, because Lang's version leaves it out** (the story of the brother, King Chasenan):

- Printed pp. 5–8: Chasenan's visit, his wife's betrayal, the hunting party, the scenes in the garden, the brothers' conversation and their return in disguise (11 paragraphs). This includes the line about "esa ligereza y liviandad que parece ser inherente al sexo frágil".

**Cut, because of the owner's rule:**

- *«á fuer de buen musulmán y»*: presents obeying an order to kill as what a good Muslim does. The sentence keeps "como vasallo sumiso y obediente".
- *«y adoptó para ello un medio muy seguro y eficaz, muy propio de las costumbres del serrallo, y de la barbarie de aquellos tiempos»*: an orientalist judgement by the translator.
- *«é hizo morir ahogadas á todas las otras mujeres de su séquito»*: extra violence, not in Lang.
- Both fables (pp. 9–12), and every line that points to them: «Te sucederá lo que al borrico…», «Merecerías… que te trataran como al asno», «será preciso hacer contigo lo que el rico labrador hizo con su mujer», «Como á esta mujer terca é imprudente debería yo tratarte», and «Ni las historias que acabáis de contarme, ni otras aun más tristes, me harán cambiar en mi designio». The second fable presents beating one's wife as good advice.

**Written for this edition** (one sentence, paragraph 3), to bridge the cut: «Pasados algunos años, el sultán Chabriar descubrió que la sultana, su esposa, le era infiel.» In the same paragraph, «El desengaño que recibió de la desenvoltura é infidelidad de la sultana agriaron su ánimo» is shortened to «El desengaño agrió su ánimo», and «Pasando al aposento de la sultana infiel, mandó cortarle la cabeza en su presencia» to «Mandó cortar la cabeza a la sultana infiel».

**Paragraphs split or joined** to match Lang's 28: the dialogue of printed pp. 8–9, set as one paragraph in 1902, is split by speaker (paragraphs 6–12); paragraph 13 joins «No lo conseguirás, ¡infeliz!» with the vizier's next warning; the sultan's answer (p. 12) is split into paragraphs 17–19, and the night scene (pp. 12–13) into paragraphs 20–28.

**For the owner to decide while proofreading:** keep the 1902 names (*Gerenarda, Diznarda, Chabriar, Chasenan*) or use the forms readers know today (*Sherezade, Dinarzada, Shahriar, Shahzamán*).
